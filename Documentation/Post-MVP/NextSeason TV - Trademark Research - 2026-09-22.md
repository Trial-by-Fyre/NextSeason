# NextSeason TV Trademark Research & Decision Record

**Trial by Fyre, LLC • September 22, 2026**

**Purpose.** This document records the trademark research and business
decision made before the initial App Store release of NextSeason TV. It
is a contemporaneous project record, not a legal opinion.

## 1. Current Decision

-   Continue using the product name NextSeason TV for the initial
    release.
-   Do not file a U.S. federal trademark application at this time.
-   Treat trademark registration as a post-launch item to revisit if the
    app develops meaningful traction.
-   Do not treat the existing NextSeasonTV.com website as a launch
    blocker based on the information currently known.
-   If the operator of NextSeasonTV.com or another claimant raises a
    trademark objection, obtain advice from a U.S. trademark attorney
    before responding substantively.

## 2. Existing Use Discovered: NextSeasonTV.com

A pre-launch clearance review identified an existing website using the
name NextSeasonTV at nextseasontv.com. The name is effectively identical
to "NextSeason TV," and the subject matter overlaps: the site publishes
information about television seasons, premiere dates, renewals,
cancellations, and related news.

This is more relevant than unrelated businesses using variations of
"Next Season" because both products concern television programs and
upcoming seasons. However, the existing site appears to be an
informational/news website, while NextSeason TV is an iOS application
centered on a personal watchlist and notifications about new seasons.

## 3. Observations About the Existing Site

-   The website remains online, but at least some content appears stale.
    For example, a search for Murdoch Mysteries surfaced its most recent
    site news from 2024 even though the series remained active and had a
    2026 season.
-   The domain registration details are privacy-redacted, but the
    available registration information indicates that the registrant is
    located in the Netherlands.
-   The site has or has had the expected social-media presence, but
    activity is uneven: the last observed Facebook post was in 2018, the
    last observed Instagram post was in 2023, and the last observed X
    post was in 2025.
-   The site apparently does not control the @nextseasontv Instagram
    handle and instead uses @nextseasoncom.
-   Based on these observations, the site does not appear to be a highly
    active or rapidly growing competing product. This is a business-risk
    observation only; it does not establish abandonment or absence of
    trademark rights.

## 4. Federal Trademark Search Summary

The research performed did not identify an obvious active U.S. federal
registration for NEXTSEASON or NEXTSEASON TV covering a
television-tracking application. Other "Next Season" marks were found in
unrelated areas such as coaching, athlete transition, and podcasting.
This result does not establish that the name is legally clear: the USPTO
database does not contain every unregistered mark, and common-law rights
can arise through use.

## 5. Relevant U.S. Trademark Principles

-   **Registration is optional.** The USPTO states that trademark rights
    can arise from use even without federal registration. Federal
    registration generally provides broader protection.
-   **Common-law rights can be geographically limited.** The USPTO
    explains that unregistered rights are based on use in commerce and
    may be enforceable only in the geographic areas where the mark is
    used.
-   **The owner's country of residence is not dispositive.** A
    Netherlands-based owner can have U.S. rights if the mark has
    acquired protectable use or recognition in U.S. commerce. The
    overseas address therefore does not by itself eliminate risk.
-   **Similarity is not the only issue.** Likelihood of confusion
    considers both the similarity of the marks and the relationship
    between the parties' goods or services. Here the names are extremely
    close and the subject matter overlaps, although the
    products/services are not identical.
-   **Federal database searching is not a complete clearance search.**
    The USPTO recommends also considering internet and other sources
    because unregistered users may possess common-law rights.

## 6. Business Risk Assessment

The team considered three practical options: rebrand before launch, seek
a trademark attorney's clearance opinion and potentially file now, or
continue with the existing name while deferring registration. Based on
the app's current stage and the evidence available, the third option was
selected.

The decision is not based on a conclusion that NextSeasonTV.com has no
U.S. rights. Rather, Trial by Fyre is accepting a degree of uncertainty
because the cost and disruption of a pre-launch rebrand or formal
trademark engagement are not currently justified by the apparent
business risk. The app has not yet established a customer base, and the
existing site does not presently appear to be an active App Store
competitor.

## 7. Domain and Branding Plan

-   getnextseason.com --- owned by Trial by Fyre, LLC.
-   getnextseasontv.com --- owned by Trial by Fyre, LLC and intended to
    be the Apple-facing domain because the App Store product name is
    NextSeason TV.
-   Both domains are intended to point to the same NextSeason TV
    website.
-   nextseasontv.com is owned/operated by the pre-existing third party
    discussed above and is not affiliated with Trial by Fyre.

## 8. When to Revisit

Revisit federal trademark registration when one or more of the following
occurs:

-   NextSeason TV develops meaningful user or subscription traction.
-   The name becomes materially valuable to Trial by Fyre's business.
-   The app expands substantially beyond the initial U.S./Canada/UK
    launch.
-   A confusingly similar app or service enters the market.
-   NextSeasonTV.com becomes substantially more active or expands into
    an app/product that more directly overlaps NextSeason TV.
-   Trial by Fyre receives any objection, demand, cease-and-desist
    communication, or other trademark-related contact.

## 9. Recommended Response to a Future Dispute

If a third party claims rights in NEXTSEASON TV or a confusingly similar
mark, preserve the communication and the research summarized here. Do
not concede infringement, promise a rename, threaten the claimant, or
make substantive legal arguments before obtaining advice from a
U.S.-licensed trademark attorney.
